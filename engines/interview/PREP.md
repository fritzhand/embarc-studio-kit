# Prepare for the studio

Start a week before the session. This part happens at your desk, and it is what makes the hour in the studio go
well. The questions are in [`QUESTIONS.md`](./QUESTIONS.md), and the session itself in
[`RUN-OF-SHOW.md`](./RUN-OF-SHOW.md).

## Watch your market

Spend an hour with what companies in your space post: competitors, companies that sell to the same customers, and
founders your customers follow. Look on LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and their websites. Save links to the
videos you watched to the end, and to the ones you skipped.

For each one, note how it opens, how long it runs, what is on screen while the founder talks, and what everyone
seems to say. Keep the list in `projects/<name>/research.md`. You will use it again when you storyboard
([`../cut/STORYBOARD.md`](../cut/STORYBOARD.md)).

## Write your answers

Write a short answer to each question, in the words you would use with a friend. Pitch-deck language sounds stiff
on camera.

Then write two lines on their own: one sentence on what your company does, and how you will close, with your
website and what you want people to do next.

## Practice out loud

Read each answer out loud and time it. Aim for 30 to 90 seconds. Record yourself on your phone and watch it back
once, then cut whatever you stumbled on or did not need.

## Memorize what you can

Learn your one-sentence description and your close word for word. For everything else, know your points and let
the wording come as you talk. Reading from a page or a screen shows in your eyes on camera.

## The day before

Send your host the questions, but not your answers, so the conversation stays fresh.

## For the agent

A founder may ask for help preparing. You can turn their notes into a draft answer for each question, time an
answer at about 150 words a minute, and tighten an answer that runs long. Keep their words and their facts: do not
add a figure, a customer, or a claim they did not give you, and do not write lines for them to read on camera.
